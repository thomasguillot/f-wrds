F Wrds replaces short triggers with longer text as you type. Type a trigger in any app and it expands on the spot. For example, `;sig` can become your full email signature. It lives in the menu bar, and you open its window from there to add and edit snippets.

F Wrds needs macOS 26 (Tahoe) or later and an Apple Silicon Mac.

## Install

F Wrds is not signed with an Apple Developer ID or notarized, so macOS blocks it the first time you open it. To allow it:

1. Double-click **F Wrds**. When macOS warns that it can't verify the developer, click **Done**, not **Move to Trash**.
2. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and scroll to the **Security** section.
3. Click **Open Anyway** next to the message that "F Wrds" was blocked to protect your Mac. Authenticate, then click **Open**.

macOS only asks once. After that the app opens normally.

## Quit other text expanders first

If another text expander is still running, both apps react to the same trigger and the text comes out garbled. Quit it before you use F Wrds. If it's Espanso, F Wrds spots it and offers to quit it for you. Coming from Espanso? You can [import your snippets](/docs/backup/#coming-from-espanso).

## First launch

On first launch, F Wrds opens a short setup with three steps.

1. **Welcome to F Wrds** explains what the app does and why it needs two permissions. Click **Continue**, or **Set Up Later** to skip.
2. **Turn on two permissions** shows live status for each one. The setup moves on by itself once both are on.
3. **Try It** gives you a text field. If you have a snippet with the trigger `;fwrds`, type it there and watch it expand. Click **Done** to open the F Wrds window.

## The two permissions

macOS asks you to allow two things. Neither is used for anything else.

- **Accessibility** lets F Wrds type the full text into the app you are using.
- **Input Monitoring** lets F Wrds see what you type, so it can notice a trigger.

F Wrds can't expand anything until both are on. If something is missing, choose **Help → Run Setup Again…**.

## Updates and permissions

macOS ties each permission to the app's code signature. Release builds are signed with the same certificate every time, so both permissions carry over when F Wrds updates itself. A build with a different signature, such as one you build from source, counts as a different app. F Wrds notices this and reopens setup at the permissions step so you can confirm both again. Your snippets and settings are not affected.

## Where your data lives

Your snippets and settings are stored in `~/Library/Application Support/FWrds/`.
